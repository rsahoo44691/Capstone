The Problem

The two errors do not cost the same

  • Coronary artery disease is a leading cause of death worldwide Screening decisions rest on a handful of measurements from one visit
  • A model can estimate risk and flag who needs further testing
  • But a screening tool must be judged on which mistakes it makes, not how many
One more test
Cost of a false positive: a healthy patient is screened again
A missed diagnosis
Cost of a false negative: a sick patient is sent home

UCI Cleveland heart disease database

Positive rate. 139 disease, 164 no disease, so near balanced

  • Historical benchmark to beat: ~77% accuracy Detrano et al. (1989), the study that published this data

Methodology

One leakage-free pipeline, reused everywhere

  • Baseline. Logistic regression. Interpretable coefficients, and the same model family as the 1989 study
  • Optimization. One-hot encoding of 8 categorical codes, plus GridSearchCV on ROC-AUC
  • Testing. Repeated cross-validation, paired significance tests, per-patient error analysis
  • Deployment. FastAPI service, model loaded once at startup, same pipeline object
80/20 stratified split · seed 42
Preprocessing fit on the training fold only
Versions pinned · data bundled · runs tracked in MLflow

What Shipped

Both findings shipped into the service

  • Threshold moved to 0.25 Zero false negatives on the held-out set
  • New requires_review flag Any prediction between 0.2 and 0.8 returns with a reason and routes to a clinician
  • FastAPI service, version 1.1.0 Pydantic validation, model loaded once at startup, no patient data in logs
  • Test suite grew from 7 to 10 All passing. Runs tracked in MLflow
An error analysis that changes nothing is an academic exercise.

These findings changed the system.

Limits and Next Steps

What I would not claim, and what comes next

  • 61 test patients. Every subgroup estimate is wide
  • 6 of 7 errors were male. A signal to monitor, not proven bias
  • Threshold tuned on the data it is measured on. Nested CV is the correct fix
  • Single site, 1989, 68% male. May not transfer to a modern population
